## Persona
Electrical engineer (PE) who designs control panels, MCCs and I/O wiring to NFPA 79 / UL 508A, new to PLC software, checking whether the ladder matches what is actually wired in the panel.

## What works (max 6 bullets)
- **found-signals**: the live-zero explanation, the NE43 idea that a dead loop must become bad quality (not a number), and the HART facts (FSK at 1200/2200 Hz, 1200 bps, tones average out) are correct and useful. The loop-budget example checks out: 800 m of 18 AWG is about 17 ohm, about 0.34 V at 20 mA.
- **found-signals**: the sinking/sourcing rule is stated correctly (PNP sources +24 V and needs a sinking card) and the "NPN on a sinking card looks dead" warning is the kind of thing that costs a day on site.
- **plc-sealin**: the NC stop read with XIC, with the reason ("a cut wire looks like a Stop press"), and the Stop-after-branch rule are the right electrical reasoning.
- **plc-safety**: the dual-channel e-stop with discrepancy time, and "Ignition reads safety tags, never writes them", match how these are built in real panels.
- **sim-scp**: the math checks out. 9830 counts gives 5.0 m, 5243 gives 1.5 m, 0 gives -2.5 m, and SCP parameter order is correct.
- **plc-analog**: the on-delay plus deadband timeline (set at 8.0, clear at 7.8) and the ST alarm example behave as described.

## Problems (max 10, most important first)
1. **[severity: high] [area: accuracy]** plc-sealin "Worked example: pump with two stops and a fault", plc-devices "The motor starter anatomy" (rung `XIC(Start) XIC(OL_OK) OTE(Contactor)`), and sim-sealin tip 2: overload is shown only as a PLC input in the coil rung. In a UL 508A / NFPA 79 panel the overload NC contact and the e-stop sit hardwired in series with the starter coil, and the PLC gets a separate auxiliary contact for status. As written, a PLC fault, a forced bit or a welded output relay can keep the coil energized with the overload open. Fix: say explicitly that overload and e-stop are hardwired in the coil circuit, that the PLC only monitors them, and add one schematic showing coil, aux and OL wiring.
2. **[severity: high] [area: accuracy]** sim-sealin tip 2 ("Add every permissive and trip in series with Stop: overload OK, guard closed, E-stop healthy") contradicts plc-safety (a software e-stop "is not a safety function") and plc-sealin ("E-stop must cut power through hardwired safety hardware"). A learner who follows the sim tip will build the unsafe pattern. Fix: reword to "process permissives in logic; overload and e-stop hardwired, PLC monitors them only."
3. **[severity: med] [area: accuracy]** found-devices objectives: "why motor starters have three separate protections". The anatomy table then lists the contactor as "Not a protection, it is a switch", so only two are protections (short-circuit via breaker/fuse, and overload). Fix: say a starter is disconnect plus short-circuit protection plus overload, and that the contactor is the switch.
4. **[severity: med] [area: accuracy]** found-signals "Scaling" section: it says "a 16-bit card where 0 to 20 mA spans 0 to 32,767 counts", but then gives 4 mA = 6,242 and 20 mA = 31,208. Full scale of 32,767 would put 4 mA at about 6,553 and 20 mA at 32,767. The same lesson's ST snippet uses 6242. The count scale also changes across lessons: 3277/16384 in plc-analog and sim-scp, 6242/31208 here. Fix: use one scale, derive every number from it, and say that the real count range is module-specific.
5. **[severity: med] [area: accuracy]** Inconsistent NAMUR thresholds. found-devices table: 3.6 mA = "Below range: transmitter is reporting a fault". found-signals NE43 table: 3.6 to 3.8 mA = "over- or under-range, not a clean fault". plc-analog: under-range "below about 3.8 mA". sim-scp brief: "below about 3.8 mA the number is not a real level", but its fault test is raw below 3000 counts, which is about 3.66 mA on the 16384 scale (3.8 mA would be about 3113 counts). Fix: one NE43 table linked from all three lessons, and set the sim threshold to match the brief.
6. **[severity: med] [area: sim]** sim-scp, fault logic: the "dead loop" is modeled as the raw count going to 0. A real Logix analog card reports wire-off and under-range as status bits on the channel, and the scaled value does not simply go to -2.5 m. Fix: add a card status tag (e.g., `LevelWireOff`) to the io list and tell the learner to use the module status bit in the explain. Keep the raw-count check as a second, module-independent test.
7. **[severity: med] [area: ui]** m-sim.png (phone 390px, Lab for "Three-wire start/stop"): the bottom tab bar sits over the brief's ladder preview mid-page. The "EDIT" button in the rung toolbar is clipped at the right edge ("EDI"). The rung editor starts well below the fold, after the full brief and both I/O cards, so on a phone the electrician has to scroll a long way to reach the ladder. Fix: collapse the brief by default on phone, keep the rung editor near the top, and fix the toolbar overflow.
8. **[severity: med] [area: accuracy]** found-signals, "Discrete signals": "PNP is the common choice in North America and Europe's modern installs". This is unsupported and is usually stated the other way round (NPN has long been the common sensor type in North American installs, PNP is dominant in Europe). Fix: cite a source, or say "PNP is common in Europe; both exist in North America."
9. **[severity: low] [area: content]** plc-analog, "HH, H, L, LL alarms", ALMA paragraph ends mid-sentence: "...or you may use A" and then the "## Practice" heading. Fix: finish the sentence.
10. **[severity: low] [area: sim]** sim-sealin seals on the `Motor` output tag, and the tip says many integrators seal on a separate internal bit because forcing the output would corrupt the seal. The challenge teaches the pattern its own tip calls fragile, with no note. Fix: either seal on `Motor_Run_Cmd` in the challenge or add a sentence tying the two.

## Missing (max 5)
- A hardwired control-circuit view: start/stop pushbuttons, the starter coil, the aux contact used for seal-in, and where the PLC output enters the circuit. The persona expects to see how the ladder rung maps to the physical coil circuit, not just to a rung.
- Output wiring for discrete outputs: how a 24 VDC output card drives a contactor coil (sourcing vs sinking outputs, interposing relays, coil suppression, output fusing). found-signals covers only inputs in depth; the output side is one sentence.
- Stop categories (0/1/2) mapped to the contactor and the normal PLC Stop. plc-safety names category 0 but nothing ties it to the seal-in lesson, where the reader most needs it.
- A step-by-step 4-20 mA loop check: meter or calibrator at the PLC terminals, expected counts, and how to log it. found-signals mentions a calibrator only in passing.
- Print reading and cross-referencing: wire numbers, terminal references, and how an I/O list relates to the ladder tag names.

## One-line verdict
Electrically sound on signals, sinking/sourcing and the sim math, but the seal-in and motor-starter lessons, and sim-sealin's own tip, put overload and e-stop in PLC logic only, which is the wrong picture for a panel reviewer; plc-intro was checked only in preview and plc-safety only by grep.
